## ⚠️ Important: On the Status of the Numbers in This Document

Every quantitative result in Part III (the empirical findings) was produced on **synthetic data** generated by stochastic-volatility and regime-switching simulators. Synthetic data was used deliberately, because it provides a known ground truth — this is what allows us to verify that an estimator recovers true structure, and to detect look-ahead leakage cleanly.

A direct consequence is that the **specific magnitudes** reported (e.g. AUC ≈ 0.97 for vol-regime classification, R² ≈ 0.89 for HAR, the drawdown-reduction percentages) are properties of the simulators, **not** guarantees about real markets. They should be re-measured on real data before being relied upon for any production or investment decision.

By contrast, the **qualitative findings** are robust and consistent with the broader academic literature:
- Look-ahead leakage produces spectacular but fake performance.
- Volatility is forecastable; returns are essentially not.
- HAR is an extremely strong volatility benchmark.
- Decomposition methods are diagnostic and risk-control tools, not sources of directional alpha.

Read the numbers as *controlled-experiment results that illustrate principles*, not as empirical facts about any traded market.

## How to Read This Document

This master SRS is organized into three parts plus a shared reference section.

**Part I — Frequency Decomposition System** specifies the analytical engine: MODWT, DWT, CWT, EMD/EEMD, VMD, the Hilbert-Huang Transform, plus the derived capabilities (Hurst estimation, wavelet coherence) and the volatility-forecasting and vol-targeting modules. This is the toolkit for *understanding the multi-scale structure* of a series.

**Part II — Pattern Detection System** specifies the named-chart-pattern detector (head-and-shoulders, flags, pennants, triangles, double tops). It reuses Part I's wavelet engine as a *denoising preprocessor* and adds a swing-extraction and geometric-matching layer on top. This is the toolkit most users mean when they say "decompose a chart into patterns."

**Part III — Empirical Findings** documents the experiments that justify the conservative design constraints imposed in Parts I and II — chiefly the mandatory causality checks, mandatory benchmarks, and the refusal to claim predictive edge.

**The relationship between the two systems:** they are complementary, not interchangeable. Frequency decomposition splits a signal into additive bands that sum back to the original, separated by timescale. Pattern detection finds localized geometric shapes that do not sum to anything and have no representation in the wavelet basis. A user may run both on the same series; Part II's denoising layer is the only point where the two share machinery.

## I.1 Introduction

### I.1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the requirements for a chart decomposition system that separates a financial price or volatility series into trend, cycle, and noise components using complementary signal-processing techniques: the Maximal Overlap Discrete Wavelet Transform (MODWT), the Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT), Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD/EEMD), Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), and the Hilbert-Huang Transform (HHT). The system also provides downstream applications: Hurst exponent estimation, wavelet coherence between two series, causal volatility forecasting, and a vol-targeting backtest with transaction costs.

The system is intended for quantitative researchers, traders, and analysts who need to (a) understand the multi-scale structure of a time series, (b) generate volatility forecasts that improve risk-sizing, and (c) backtest the resulting strategies with proper causal hygiene.

### I.1.2 Scope

The system shall:

- Decompose a single univariate price/return/volatility series into scale-localized components using MODWT, EMD, EEMD, and VMD.
- Produce two-dimensional time-frequency representations (CWT scalogram and Hilbert spectrum).
- Estimate long-memory parameters (Hurst exponent) via wavelet variance.
- Compute wavelet coherence between two series, including phase information.
- Provide hybrid pipelines that combine wavelet denoising, mode decomposition, and Hilbert spectral analysis.
- Support causally honest walk-forward forecasting of log-realized-volatility using HAR and VMD-augmented HAR models.
- Implement a vol-targeting strategy backtest with transaction costs.

The system shall **not**:

- Provide direct market-data ingestion (the user supplies arrays).
- Trade live capital or place orders.
- Make investment recommendations.

### I.1.3 Definitions, Acronyms, Abbreviations

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| MODWT | Maximal Overlap Discrete Wavelet Transform — translation-invariant DWT |
| DWT | Discrete Wavelet Transform |

## I.2 Overall Description

### I.2.1 Product Perspective

The system is a self-contained Python library plus a thin CLI/notebook layer. It depends only on widely available scientific-Python packages and a small number of decomposition libraries. It is designed to be used:

- Interactively from a Jupyter notebook for exploratory analysis.
- Scripted in batch mode for nightly backtests or scheduled vol-forecast updates.
- As an importable package from a downstream trading or risk system.

### I.2.2 Product Functions

At a high level the system provides eight functional modules:

1. **Decomposition** — MODWT, EMD, EEMD, VMD of a 1-D series.
2. **Time-frequency views** — CWT scalogram, HHT spectrum, MODWT-Hilbert spectrum.
3. **Long-memory estimation** — Hurst exponent via wavelet variance.
4. **Bivariate analysis** — wavelet coherence, cross-wavelet phase.
5. **Hybrid pipelines** — wavelet-denoise → EMD; EMD → CWT-per-IMF; MODWT → Hilbert.
6. **Realized volatility computation** from intraday returns.
7. **Forecasting** — walk-forward HAR and VMD-augmented HAR for log-RV.
8. **Strategy evaluation** — vol-targeting backtest with transaction costs.

### I.2.3 User Classes and Characteristics

| User class | Description | Key needs |
|---|---|---|
| Quantitative researcher | Explores time-series structure | Diagnostics, plots, parameter flexibility |
| Vol forecaster / risk manager | Produces daily vol forecasts | Causal pipelines, OOS metrics, robust defaults |
| Strategy developer | Builds vol-targeting systems | Backtest module, transaction-cost handling, Sharpe/QLIKE diagnostics |

### I.2.4 Operating Environment

- Python 3.10+
- Linux, macOS, or Windows
- NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, scikit-learn, PyWavelets, EMD-signal (PyEMD), vmdpy
- Optional: Jupyter for interactive notebooks

### I.2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints

The design is constrained by lessons learned in prior research:

**C1. Strict causality.** Every forecasting and feature-computation path must use only data available up to the prediction time. Decompositions applied to a full series and then sliced into "past" and "future" windows are forbidden in the forecasting module. Diagnostic modules may decompose the full series but must clearly label outputs as non-causal.

**C2. Boundary handling.** All time-frequency views must overlay or otherwise report the cone of influence so users can distinguish edge-contaminated regions from reliable ones.

**C3. Mode-ordering stability.** Data-adaptive decompositions (EMD, EEMD, VMD) reorder components run-to-run. The implementation must sort modes by a stable criterion (e.g. center frequency for VMD; dominant period for EMD) before any feature is extracted across multiple decompositions.

**C4. Benchmark gating.** The forecasting module must always evaluate a fancy model against (a) a lag-return naive forecaster and (b) HAR. A fancy model is reported as "improving" only if it beats both by a statistically significant Diebold-Mariano margin.

**C5. Reproducibility.** All stochastic routines (EEMD noise, synthetic data, train/test sampling) accept a seed argument.

### I.2.6 Assumptions and Dependencies

- The input series is uniformly sampled (daily by default).
- For realized-vol computation, intraday returns are provided as a 2-D array of shape (days, returns_per_day).
- The user is responsible for handling missing data, holidays, and time-zone alignment.

## I.3 Specific Requirements

### I.3.1 Module: Decomposition

#### I.3.1.1 MODWT (FR-DECOMP-MODWT)

The system **shall** implement MODWT decomposition with the following behavior:

- **Input:** a 1-D NumPy array `x` of length N, a wavelet name (default `'sym8'`), and a level `J` (default 6).
- **Output:** a list `[A_J, D_J, D_{J-1}, ..., D_1]` where every component has length N.
- **Normalization:** energy-preserving (i.e. `pywt.swt(norm=True)`).
- **Boundary handling:** symmetric reflection by default; configurable to periodic or zero-padding.
- **Cone of influence:** for each level *j*, return the per-sample width `coi_width(j) = (2^j - 1) * (L - 1)` where L is the wavelet filter length.
- **Power-of-two restriction:** if N is not a power of 2, the implementation **shall** pad to the next power of 2, run SWT, then trim back to N.

#### I.3.1.2 EMD and EEMD (FR-DECOMP-EMD)

- **Input:** a 1-D NumPy array `x`.
- **Output:** an array of shape `(K, N)` where the last row is the monotonic residue and rows 0..K-2 are IMFs ordered from highest to lowest frequency.
- **Stopping criterion:** Cauchy-style sifting with configurable tolerance and maximum sift iterations.
- **EEMD:** the system **shall** support an ensemble variant that runs EMD on `n_trials` noise-perturbed copies of the signal (default n_trials = 100, noise_width = 0.2 × std(x)) and averages the resulting IMFs.
- **Reporting:** for each IMF the system **shall** report a dominant period estimated via zero-crossing counting: `period = 2 * N / n_zero_crossings`.

#### I.3.1.3 VMD (FR-DECOMP-VMD)

- **Input:** a 1-D NumPy array `x`, the number of modes `K` (default 5), and a bandwidth constraint `alpha` (default 1500).
- **Output:** an array of shape `(K, N)` of modes, and an array of length K of center frequencies.
- **Mode sorting:** the system **shall** sort modes by ascending center frequency before returning.
- **Mean handling:** the system **shall** subtract the input mean before decomposition and store it for reconstruction.
- **Reconstruction error:** the system **shall** report the relative L2 reconstruction error.

### I.3.2 Module: Time-Frequency Views

#### I.3.2.1 CWT Scalogram (FR-TF-CWT)

- **Input:** a 1-D series, scale array (default `geomspace(2, 256, 64)`), and wavelet (default complex Morlet `'cmor1.5-1.0'`).
- **Output:** a 2-D power array `|W|²` of shape `(n_scales, N)`, a frequency vector, and the cone-of-influence boundary as a 1-D array indexed by time.
- **Phase:** the system **shall** return phase `angle(W)` as a separate array on request.
- **Visualization:** the system **shall** provide a plotting function that draws the scalogram on a log-period y-axis with the COI overlaid as a dashed line.

#### I.3.2.2 Hilbert Spectrum (FR-TF-HHT)

- **Input:** an array of IMFs (or MODWT detail bands) of shape `(K, N)`.
- **Method:** for each component, compute the Hilbert analytic signal `z = c + i·H(c)`, then instantaneous amplitude `A(t) = |z(t)|` and instantaneous frequency `f(t) = (1/2π)·d(arg z)/dt` via phase unwrapping.
- **Output:** a 2-D energy array `(n_freq_bins, N)` accumulated from `A(t)²` placed at the bin containing `f(t)` for each (component, time).
- **Smoothing:** the system **shall** apply a small Gaussian filter (default `sigma=(1.5, 4)`) before returning, with smoothing parameters configurable.

#### I.3.2.3 MODWT-Hilbert Spectrum (FR-TF-MODWT-HHT)

- The system **shall** support computing the Hilbert spectrum from MODWT detail bands as an alternative to EMD IMFs. This is the recommended production-friendly time-frequency view (per the hybrid pipeline in Section 3.5).

### I.3.3 Module: Long-Memory Estimation

#### I.3.3.1 Hurst via Wavelet Variance (FR-HURST)

- **Input:** a 1-D series, a wavelet (default `'db4'`), and a level range `(j_lo, j_hi)`.
- **Method:** compute MODWT to max level, then for each level *j* compute `Var(d_j)`. Regress `log₂ Var(d_j)` on `j` over `[j_lo, j_hi]`. The Hurst exponent is `H = (slope + 2) / 2` (this normalization accounts for the energy-preserving MODWT).
- **Output:** point estimate Ĥ, the slope, intercept, full variance array, and the included scale mask.
- **Diagnostics:** the system **shall** produce a log-log plot of variance vs scale with the fitted line overlaid, so users can detect kinks (scale-dependent dynamics).
- **Validation:** the implementation **shall** be unit-tested against fractional Gaussian noise of known H ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85}, with target estimation error ≤ 0.07.

### I.3.4 Module: Bivariate Analysis

#### I.3.4.1 Wavelet Coherence (FR-COH)

- **Input:** two 1-D series of equal length, scale array, wavelet (complex Morlet recommended).
- **Method:**
  - Compute CWTs `W_x`, `W_y`.
  - Form cross-wavelet `W_xy = W_x · conj(W_y)`.
  - Smooth `W_xy`, `|W_x|²`, `|W_y|²` in time (Gaussian with σ proportional to scale) and across scales.
  - Coherence: `R²(a,b) = |S(W_xy)|² / (S(|W_x|²) · S(|W_y|²))`.
- **Output:** R² array of shape `(n_scales, N)` bounded in [0, 1], phase array `angle(S(W_xy))`, frequency vector, and COI.
- **Visualization:** the system **shall** provide a heatmap function that overlays phase arrows (right = in-phase, left = anti-phase, up = y leads, down = x leads) only where coherence exceeds a configurable threshold (default 0.6) and the point is outside the COI.

### I.3.5 Module: Hybrid Pipelines

The system **shall** provide three named hybrid pipelines:

#### I.3.5.1 Hybrid A — Wavelet Denoise → EMD (FR-HYB-A)

- Apply wavelet decomposition, zero out the finest `k_drop` detail bands (default 2), reconstruct, then run EMD on the reconstruction.
- Use case: cleaner IMFs by removing noise-induced spurious extrema.

#### I.3.5.2 Hybrid B — EMD → CWT-per-IMF (FR-HYB-B)

- Run EMD, then compute a CWT scalogram of each individual IMF.
- Use case: detect residual mode mixing within an IMF.

#### I.3.5.3 Hybrid C — MODWT → Hilbert Spectrum (FR-HYB-C)

- Run MODWT, then apply Hilbert spectral analysis to each detail band.
- Use case: production-friendly time-frequency view with no mode mixing and full reproducibility.

### I.3.6 Module: Realized Volatility

#### I.3.6.1 RV Computation (FR-RV)

- **Input:** intraday returns of shape `(N_days, M_intraday)`.
- **Output:** for each day, realized variance `RV = sum(r_intraday²)`, realized vol `sqrt(RV * 252)`, log-RV `log(RV + eps)`.
- The system **shall** also accept a 1-D daily returns array, in which case RV is approximated as `r²` with a warning that this is a high-noise estimator.

### I.3.7 Module: Forecasting

#### I.3.7.1 HAR Baseline (FR-FCST-HAR)

- **Features at time t:** `[log_rv[t], mean(log_rv[t-4:t+1]), mean(log_rv[t-21:t+1])]`.
- **Model:** ridge regression with configurable regularization (default α = 5).
- **Target:** `log_rv[t+1]` (one-step-ahead).
- **Refit cadence:** every `REFIT_MOD` days (default 60), trained on all data with timestamp strictly less than t.

#### I.3.7.2 VMD-Augmented HAR (FR-FCST-VMD-HAR)

- **Causal VMD:** at each prediction time t, run VMD on a trailing window of length `WINDOW` (default 1000) of log-RV up to and including time t.
- **Refit cadence for VMD:** every `REFIT_VMD` days (default 20). Modes are reused between refits.
- **Mode sorting:** sort by ascending center frequency at every refit (Constraint C3).
- **Edge lag:** read mode values at relative index `(t - lo) - EDGE_LAG` (default EDGE_LAG = 5) to avoid the unstable VMD right edge.
- **Per-mode features:** for each of K modes, append `[m[t-lag], mean(m[t-lag-4:t-lag+1]), mean(m[t-lag-21:t-lag+1])]`.
- **Composition:** the final feature vector concatenates the 3 HAR features with the 3K VMD features.
- **Model:** ridge regression refit every `REFIT_MOD` days.

#### I.3.7.3 Evaluation (FR-FCST-EVAL)

The system **shall** compute and report, on the OOS test window:

- RMSE and MAE on log-RV.
- R² on log-RV.
- QLIKE on variance scale: `mean(σ²_true/σ²_pred − log(σ²_true/σ²_pred) − 1)`.
- Diebold-Mariano statistic comparing the candidate model's squared errors to HAR's, using Newey-West HAC standard errors with bandwidth ≥ 22 days.

A model is reported as "improves on HAR" only if **all** of the following hold:
- ΔRMSE > 0 (lower is better).
- ΔQLIKE > 0.
- |DM statistic| > 1.96 in favor of the model.

### I.3.8 Module: Vol-Targeting Backtest

#### I.3.8.1 Position Sizing (FR-BT-POS)

At end of day t, position `w[t]` for day t+1 is computed from forecast `σ̂[t+1]` as:

```
w[t] = clip(target_ann_vol / max(σ̂[t+1], eps), -MAX_LEV, +MAX_LEV)
```

Defaults: `target_ann_vol = 0.15`, `MAX_LEV = 3.0`.

#### I.3.8.2 PnL Accounting (FR-BT-PNL)

- Gross PnL on day t+1: `w[t] * ret[t+1]`.
- Turnover on day t+1: `|w[t+1] - w[t]|`.
- Transaction cost on day t+1: `turnover * BPS_COST / 10000` (default `BPS_COST = 2`).
- Net PnL: gross minus cost.

#### I.3.8.3 Backtest Metrics (FR-BT-METRICS)

The system **shall** report, over the evaluation window:

- Annualized return and vol.
- Sharpe ratio.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Average gross leverage.
- Annualized turnover.
- Cost drag (gross return − net return, annualized).

The system **shall** also produce three plots:

1. Cumulative net PnL vs buy-and-hold and naive vol-target.
2. 30-day rolling realized strategy vol vs target line.
3. Position size over time.

### I.3.9 Non-Functional Requirements

#### I.3.9.1 Performance

- **NFR-P1:** A single MODWT decomposition of a length-2048 series shall complete in < 100 ms on commodity hardware.
- **NFR-P2:** A single VMD decomposition (K = 5, length 1024) shall complete in < 2 seconds.
- **NFR-P3:** A 2000-day walk-forward HAR backtest with refits every 60 days shall complete in < 10 seconds end-to-end.
- **NFR-P4:** A 2000-day walk-forward VMD-HAR backtest with VMD refits every 20 days shall complete in < 5 minutes.

#### I.3.9.2 Reliability

- **NFR-R1:** All decomposition functions shall validate input length, dtype, and NaN content, raising informative errors before invoking the underlying algorithm.
- **NFR-R2:** The forecasting module shall fail loudly (raise an exception) if the user attempts to fit on data that includes timestamps ≥ the prediction time.

#### I.3.9.3 Usability

- **NFR-U1:** Every public function shall have a docstring with: purpose, parameters with types and defaults, return value description, an example, and a "Pitfalls" section where relevant.
- **NFR-U2:** The library shall ship with three Jupyter notebooks: (a) decomposition tour, (b) forecasting walkthrough, (c) vol-targeting backtest.

#### I.3.9.4 Reproducibility

- **NFR-RP1:** Every stochastic routine accepts a `seed` argument.
- **NFR-RP2:** The library shall log its version and the version of all decomposition dependencies into any saved result.

## I.5 Validation and Test Requirements

### I.5.1 Unit Tests

Each decomposition module **shall** have tests covering:

- **Round-trip:** sum of components reconstructs the input to within a documented tolerance (1e-10 for MODWT, 1e-6 for EMD, 0.2 relative error for VMD).
- **Translation invariance** (MODWT only): shifting the input shifts every component by the same amount.
- **Mode count and ordering:** VMD returns K modes sorted by ascending center frequency.
- **Edge robustness:** decompositions on series of length 100, 257, 1024, and 5000 all complete without error.

### I.5.2 Statistical Validation

- **Hurst estimator** shall be validated on fractional Gaussian noise with H ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85}. Each test uses N = 4096 samples; estimation error must be ≤ 0.07 (95% confidence by replication over 50 seeds).
- **Wavelet coherence** shall be validated on a known-construction pair of series (shared slow factor + independent fast noise) with a deliberate decoupling window. The test must show R² > 0.5 at long scales outside the decoupling window and R² < 0.3 inside it.

### I.5.3 Causality Tests

- **No-leakage test:** for every walk-forward forecast, swap the target series at time t with random noise for all t' < t and verify the predictions at t' do not change. Failure indicates the model is using post-t information.
- **Look-ahead detection:** the test suite shall include a deliberate non-causal forecaster and verify its OOS metrics are absurdly good (R² > 0.95), as a regression test against accidentally reintroducing leakage.

### I.5.4 End-to-End Test

A reference end-to-end test **shall** generate a 2500-day Heston SV path, run the full pipeline (decomposition diagnostics → log-RV → HAR and VMD-HAR forecasts → vol-targeting backtest), and check:

- HAR achieves R² ≥ 0.85 on log-RV out-of-sample.
- HAR's vol-targeted strategy realizes 30-day rolling vol within ±30% of the target band on at least 70% of days.
- VMD-HAR's RMSE is within ±10% of HAR's RMSE (i.e. neither catastrophically worse, nor heroically better — guard against accidentally reintroducing look-ahead).

## I.B Known Limitations and Implementation Traps

**B1. Look-ahead leakage is the #1 trap.** Any decomposition computed on the full series and then "used to predict" past values is leaking future information. The implementation must structurally prevent this — for example by making the forecasting module accept only a function `(data_up_to_t) -> features`, not a precomputed feature matrix.

**B2. VMD mode reordering between refits.** When VMD is refit on a sliding window, the algorithm's local optima may reorder modes. The implementation must sort by center frequency at every refit, or the model's feature vector silently changes meaning.

**B3. EMD end effects.** Cubic-spline envelopes near the boundary are unstable. Recent IMF values may shift significantly when new data arrives. The forecasting module's `edge_lag` parameter exists for this reason; do not set it to 0 without explicit justification.

**B4. VMD reconstruction is approximate.** Unlike MODWT, VMD does not satisfy exact reconstruction. Treat its modes as a regularized decomposition, not an orthogonal one. Do not interpret variance shares as if they sum to 100%.

**B5. Returns are nearly unforecastable.** The forecasting module's target should be log-RV, not next-day return. Backtests forecasting return direction with decomposition features generally produce no edge once causality is enforced.

**B6. HAR is a brutal benchmark.** Most fancy decomposition-based forecasters will tie or slightly underperform HAR with ridge regression. Gains typically require nonlinear models or cross-asset features. The implementation must always report HAR side-by-side with any "improved" model.

**B7. Cone of influence is non-negotiable in time-frequency plots.** Users will misinterpret edge artifacts as real features. Every CWT scalogram, HHT spectrum, and coherence plot must show the COI.

**B8. Wavelet choice is not neutral.** Different mother wavelets give qualitatively different decompositions, particularly at short scales. The system should let users supply their own wavelet but document the default's properties and provide a sanity-check function that recomputes the decomposition with a different wavelet and reports the cross-correlation per band.

# Part II — Pattern Detection System

*(Originally Part II. Two-layer architecture: wavelet denoising — reusing Part I — followed by swing extraction and geometric pattern matching for head-and-shoulders, flags, pennants, triangles, and double tops.)*

---

## II.1 Introduction

### II.1.1 Purpose

This document specifies a system that detects **named technical chart patterns** — head-and-shoulders, flags, pennants, triangles, double tops/bottoms, and trend channels — in a price series. The system uses a **two-layer architecture**: a wavelet-denoising preprocessing layer (carried over from the frequency-decomposition work of v1.0) followed by a geometric pattern-detection layer that operates on a swing-point skeleton.

This is a fundamentally different task from the additive frequency decomposition described in Part I. Frequency decomposition splits a series into trend/cycle/noise bands that sum back to the original. Pattern detection identifies localized *geometric shapes* that do not sum to anything and have no representation in the wavelet basis. The wavelet layer here serves only as **noise reduction**, not as the analysis itself.

### II.1.2 Scope

The system **shall**:

- Denoise a price series using wavelet thresholding to produce a clean skeleton suitable for swing detection.
- Extract swing points (alternating significant highs and lows) from the denoised series.
- Detect a configurable set of named chart patterns via geometric rules over the swing skeleton.
- Report each detection with location, the constituent swing points, and a confidence/quality score.
- Quantify false-positive rates against a random-walk null.
- Visualize the denoised series, the swing skeleton, and the highlighted detections.

The system **shall not**:

- Claim predictive edge for any pattern without out-of-sample validation against a null.
- Place orders or manage capital.
- Ingest market data directly (the user supplies arrays).

### II.1.3 Relationship to Part I

The wavelet decomposition module (MODWT, DWT) from Part I is **reused** here as the denoising layer. The frequency-decomposition outputs (component bands, scalograms, Hurst, coherence) remain available as a separate analytical capability but are **not** part of the pattern-detection data flow. A user may run both subsystems on the same series for complementary insight.

### II.1.4 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Swing point / pivot | A significant local high or low that anchors pattern geometry |
| Skeleton | The piecewise-linear series connecting consecutive swing points |
| ZigZag | A swing-extraction method using a reversal-threshold rule |
| Pivot prominence | The price distance from a pivot to its neighboring pivots |
| H&S | Head and Shoulders: five-pivot pattern H-L-H-L-H with the middle H highest |
| Flag / Pennant | A sharp directional "pole" followed by a small counter-trend consolidation |
| Neckline | The line connecting the two troughs of a head-and-shoulders |
| False-positive rate (FPR) | Fraction of detections on a random-walk null that the system flags as patterns |

## II.2 Overall Description

### II.2.1 Architecture — Two Layers

```
   raw price series
        │
        ▼
 ┌──────────────────────┐
 │ LAYER 1: DENOISING    │  wavelet thresholding (reuse Part I MODWT/DWT)
 │  - drop finest bands  │  output: smooth skeleton-ready series
 └──────────────────────┘
        │
        ▼
 ┌──────────────────────┐
 │ LAYER 2: PATTERN       │
 │ DETECTION             │
 │  2a. swing extraction  │  robust extrema → alternating H/L skeleton
 │  2b. geometric matchers│  rule-based detectors per pattern type
 │  2c. scoring + FPR     │  quality score, null-comparison
 └──────────────────────┘
        │
        ▼
   detections + visualization
```

### II.2.2 Critical Design Lessons (from prototype)

The prototype implementation surfaced lessons that are now binding requirements:

**L1 — Denoising strength and swing threshold are coupled.** Light denoising + tight threshold yields hundreds of spurious pivots; heavy denoising + loose threshold flattens real patterns out of existence. The two parameters MUST be tunable jointly and MUST be validated against labeled or planted examples. Neither has a universal default; defaults are starting points only.

**L2 — Use vetted extrema primitives.** Hand-rolled ZigZag logic is error-prone (the prototype had two distinct reset-logic bugs that silently produced wrong pivots). The swing extractor SHALL be built on a well-tested primitive (e.g. `scipy.signal.argrelextrema`) with an explicit comparison-window parameter, plus an alternation-enforcement and prominence-filter pass.

**L3 — False positives dominate.** Detecting genuine patterns is easy; not firing on random structure that resembles a pattern is hard. The system MUST measure false-positive rate against a random-walk null and report it alongside any detection summary.

**L4 — Thresholds must be scale-robust.** A percentage threshold breaks when the series has quiet and volatile regimes (the prototype missed a planted pattern in a quiet zone because a volatility-scaled threshold was dragged down). Thresholds SHALL be expressible as a fraction of the series' total range, or volatility-normalized per-regime, not a single global percentage.

|---|---|

| Quant researcher | FPR metrics, batch detection, labeled-data validation harness |
| Strategy developer | Detections as timestamped events with scores for backtesting |

## II.3 Specific Requirements

### II.3.1 Layer 1 — Denoising

#### FR-DN-1 Wavelet denoise
- **Input:** price series `x` (length N), wavelet (default `'sym8'`), decomposition level (default 4), number of finest detail bands to drop `k_drop` (default 1–2).
- **Method:** wavelet decompose, zero the `k_drop` finest detail bands, reconstruct, trim to N.
- **Output:** denoised series of length N.
- **Alternative:** soft/hard thresholding of detail coefficients (universal threshold `σ√(2 ln N)`) SHALL be offered as an option to zeroing whole bands.

#### FR-DN-2 Denoise–swing coupling
- The system SHALL provide a single calibration routine that jointly sweeps `k_drop` and the swing threshold, reporting swing count and (where labels exist) detection recall, so the user can pick a coupled operating point (L1).

### II.3.2 Layer 2a — Swing Extraction

#### FR-SW-1 Extrema detection
- **Method:** local maxima/minima via `argrelextrema` with a comparison-window `order` parameter (default 10), NOT a hand-rolled loop (L2).
- **Alternation enforcement:** collapse consecutive same-type extrema, keeping the more extreme one, to guarantee strict H/L/H/L alternation.
- **Prominence filter:** drop any pivot whose price distance to the previous retained pivot is below `min_prominence` (default = 8% of series range) (L4).
- **Minimum spacing:** enforce a minimum index gap between consecutive pivots (default 8 samples).

#### FR-SW-2 Output
- Ordered list of `(index, kind)` where `kind ∈ {'H','L'}`, strictly alternating.
- The piecewise-linear skeleton connecting these pivots.

#### FR-SW-3 Threshold robustness
- The swing threshold SHALL be specifiable as: (a) a fraction of total price range, (b) a multiple of rolling ATR, or (c) an absolute price value. Percentage-of-price SHALL NOT be the only option (L4).

### II.3.3 Layer 2b — Pattern Detectors

Each detector consumes the swing list `(piv, kinds)` and returns zero or more detections `(start_idx, end_idx, pattern_name, score)`.

#### FR-PAT-HS Head and Shoulders
- Match the label sequence `H L H L H` over 5 consecutive pivots.
- Require: middle peak (head) strictly highest; left/right shoulders within `tol_shoulder` (default 0.35) relative difference; the two troughs (neckline) within `tol_neckline` (default 0.5).
- Inverse H&S: the mirror pattern `L H L H L` with the middle trough lowest.

#### FR-PAT-FLAG Flag / Pennant
- Identify a "pole": a single L→H advance exceeding `pole_min` (default 40% of series range).
- Identify a consolidation: subsequent move retracing only `0 < r < retr_max` (default 0.6) of the pole, staying above the pole's base.
- Flag = parallel channel consolidation; Pennant = converging consolidation. The detector SHALL distinguish them by fitting upper/lower bounds to the consolidation pivots and testing for parallel vs converging slopes.

#### FR-PAT-TRI Triangles
- Ascending: flat highs, rising lows. Descending: falling highs, flat lows. Symmetric: converging highs and lows. Detect by linear-fitting the highs and lows over a pivot window and classifying the slope pair.

#### FR-PAT-DT Double Top / Bottom
- Double top: `H L H` with the two highs within `tol_equal` (default 0.03) and a sufficient intervening trough. Double bottom is the mirror.

#### FR-PAT-EXT Extensibility
- New detectors SHALL be addable via a registry interface: a function `(piv, kinds, x, params) -> list[Detection]`. The framework SHALL run all registered detectors and aggregate results.

### II.3.4 Layer 2c — Scoring and Validation

#### FR-SCORE-1 Quality score
- Each detection SHALL carry a score in [0, 1] reflecting how closely the geometry matches the ideal (e.g. shoulder symmetry, neckline flatness, pole steepness). The scoring function is pattern-specific and documented per detector.

#### FR-FPR-1 Null comparison (MANDATORY)
- The system SHALL provide a routine that runs the full pipeline on `n_null` (default 1000) random-walk series matched to the target's length and volatility, and reports the per-pattern false-positive rate (detections per series).
- Any detection summary presented to a user SHALL include the corresponding null FPR so a detection's significance can be judged (L3).

#### FR-FPR-2 Significance gating
- A detection MAY be flagged "noteworthy" only if its quality score exceeds the 95th percentile of scores produced on the null series for the same pattern.

### II.3.5 Visualization

#### FR-VIZ-1 Three-panel output
- Panel 1: raw vs denoised series.
- Panel 2: swing skeleton on raw and on denoised (to show pivot reduction).
- Panel 3: denoised series with detected patterns shaded and constituent pivots marked.

#### FR-VIZ-2 Detection report
- A tabular report: pattern name, start/end dates, score, null-FPR percentile.

### II.3.6 Optional ML Extension (Future)

#### FR-ML-1 Window classifier (informative, not v2.0-mandatory)
- A sliding-window classifier MAY be offered as an alternative/supplement to geometric rules: render each window as an image and classify with a CNN, or classify the numeric window with a sequence model. This requires labeled training data and is out of scope for the Part II deliverable but the API SHALL leave room for it (the detector registry of FR-PAT-EXT accommodates ML detectors).

### II.3.7 Non-Functional Requirements

- **NFR-P1:** Full pipeline (denoise + swings + all detectors) on a 1000-point series SHALL run in < 500 ms.
- **NFR-P2:** The null-FPR routine with 1000 surrogates SHALL run in < 60 s.
- **NFR-R1:** Swing extraction SHALL be built on vetted primitives, not bespoke loops (L2).
- **NFR-R2:** All thresholds SHALL default to scale-robust (range- or volatility-normalized) forms (L4).
- **NFR-U1:** Every detector SHALL document its geometric rule, parameters, and a worked example.
- **NFR-RP1:** The null-surrogate generator SHALL accept a seed.

## II.5 Validation and Test Requirements

### II.5.1 Planted-Pattern Tests (MANDATORY)
- The test suite SHALL construct synthetic series with deliberately planted patterns (H&S, flag, triangle, double top) at known locations, run the pipeline, and assert each planted pattern is detected within a tolerance window of its true location.

### II.5.2 False-Positive Tests (MANDATORY)
- Run the pipeline on pure random-walk series and assert the per-pattern detection rate is below a documented ceiling (e.g. < 0.5 H&S detections per 500-point random walk at default settings).

### II.5.3 Threshold-Robustness Tests
- Construct a series with a quiet regime and a volatile regime, each containing a planted pattern. Assert both are detected — verifying the threshold does not get dragged to a single global value that misses the quiet-regime pattern (L4).

### II.5.4 Determinism Test
- With a fixed seed, the null-FPR routine SHALL produce identical results across runs.

## II.B Known Limitations and Traps

**B1 — Denoise/threshold coupling (L1).** No universal setting exists. Calibrate jointly per asset class and per target pattern timescale.

**B2 — Brittleness of geometric rules.** Real patterns rarely match textbook geometry. Tolerances are unavoidable and every tolerance loosening raises the false-positive rate. There is no tolerance that is simultaneously sensitive and specific.

**B3 — False positives are the core problem (L3).** Always report null FPR. A detection without a null comparison is not interpretable.

**B4 — Predictive value is contested.** The academic literature (e.g. Lo, Mamaysky & Wang 2000) finds at best marginal out-of-sample information in classical patterns once data-snooping and costs are accounted for. This system DETECTS patterns; it does not assert they predict returns. Any trading use MUST be validated against a benchmark with transaction costs, exactly as in the volatility-forecasting discipline.

**B5 — Wavelets are preprocessing only.** The wavelet layer reduces noise so swing detection is stable. It does NOT find or define patterns. Do not conflate the Part I frequency-decomposition outputs with pattern detection.

**B6 — Look-ahead in live use.** For real-time detection, denoising at the right edge suffers the same boundary problem documented in Part I (cone of influence). A pattern detected using the most recent points may change as new data arrives. Causal/streaming denoising and an "unconfirmed near the edge" flag are required for any live deployment.

# Part III — Empirical Findings

*(The experimental evidence base. All results are from synthetic data — see the disclaimer at the top of this document.)*

---

## III.1 Why This Document Exists

The SRS documents impose several constraints that may look overly conservative — mandatory causality checks, mandatory benchmarks, mandatory null comparisons, and a refusal to claim predictive edge. This appendix records the experiments that motivated each constraint. The headline conclusion:

> **Simple baselines (HAR for volatility, vol-targeting for position sizing, lag-returns for direction) capture most of the extractable signal in financial time series. Wavelet/EMD/VMD decomposition earns its keep as diagnostic and risk-control tooling, not as a source of directional alpha.**

Every experiment used synthetic data because synthetic data has a *known ground truth*, which lets us verify whether an estimator recovers the true structure and whether an apparent edge is real or an artifact.

---

## III.2 Experiment 1 — The Look-Ahead Leakage Trap

**Setup.** MODWT decomposition of a price series, used to build features for a one-day-ahead return forecaster (ridge regression). Two versions: one decomposing the full series once then slicing (the common but wrong approach), one recomputing the decomposition causally at each prediction time.

**Result.**

| Method | Information Coefficient | Sharpe | Hit Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| MODWT with look-ahead (leaked) | +0.988 | +17.4 | 94.3% |
| MODWT done causally (honest) | −0.037 | −0.65 | 47.7% |
| Lag-returns benchmark | −0.031 | −0.19 | 48.6% |

**Interpretation.** The leaked version looks like a money-printing strategy. It is pure data leakage: when the wavelet transform is computed on the full series, the coefficient at time *t* incorporates values from *t+1, t+2, …*, so the "feature" already knows the answer. The causal version is statistically indistinguishable from the lag-return baseline.

**Constraint motivated.** Part I §C1 (strict causality), and Part II's no-leakage regression test: swap past target values for noise and verify predictions at those times do not change.

---

## III.3 Experiment 2 — Volatility Is Forecastable, Returns Are Not

**Setup.** Heston stochastic-volatility path simulated at intraday frequency (78 returns/day), aggregated to daily realized variance. Autocorrelation of returns vs log-realized-volatility measured directly.

**Result.**

| Series | Lag-1 ACF | Lag-22 ACF | Lag-100 ACF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Returns | 0.04 | ~0 | ~0 |
| Log-realized-volatility | 0.94 | 0.62 | 0.23 |

Hurst exponent (wavelet-variance estimator, validated on fractional Gaussian noise to within 0.07):
- Returns: H ≈ 0.5–0.57 (memoryless / random-walk-like)
- |Returns| and squared returns (vol proxies): H ≈ 0.84–0.88 (strong long memory)

**Interpretation.** Returns carry essentially no linear predictable structure; volatility carries a great deal. This is *the* reason every productive experiment in this session targeted volatility rather than price/return direction.

**Constraint motivated.** Part I B5 (forecast log-RV, not returns).

---

## III.4 Experiment 3 — HAR Is a Brutal Benchmark

**Setup.** Walk-forward one-step-ahead forecasting of log-realized-volatility. Compared HAR (3 features: daily/weekly/monthly averages of log-RV) against a causal VMD-augmented HAR (HAR features + per-mode features from a sliding-window VMD, modes sorted by center frequency, edge-lag 5). VMD refit every 20 days; models refit every 60 days.

**Result (OOS, 1749 days).**

| Model | RMSE | R² | QLIKE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive (random walk) | 0.2552 | 0.874 | 0.0330 |
| HAR | 0.2398 | 0.889 | 0.0292 |
| HAR + VMD | 0.2507 | 0.878 | 0.0317 |

VMD-augmented vs HAR: ΔRMSE −4.6%, Diebold-Mariano stat −2.74 (HAR significantly *better*).

A first, naive VMD-HAR attempt (before sorting modes by frequency) scored R² = 0.01 — catastrophically worse — because the VMD modes silently reordered between refits, so the feature vector changed meaning every 20 days.

**Interpretation.** HAR's three-feature parsimony captures nearly all the predictable structure in log-RV. Adding VMD features didn't help and, when modes weren't stabilized, actively destroyed the model. The improvements claimed in much of the literature come from (a) look-ahead leakage, (b) in-sample evaluation, (c) weak benchmarks like AR(1), or (d) deep nonlinear models where gains come from nonlinearity rather than the decomposition.

**Constraints motivated.** Part I C3 (mode-ordering stability), C4 (benchmark gating), B6 (HAR is a brutal benchmark), and the end-to-end acceptance test requiring VMD-HAR to land within ±10% of HAR (guard against re-introduced leakage).

---

## III.5 Experiment 4 — Vol-Targeting Is Where Vol Forecasts Pay Off

**Setup.** Vol-targeting position sizing (position ∝ target_vol / forecast_vol, capped, 2bps/turnover cost) using different vol forecasts, vs buy-and-hold.

**Result.**

| Strategy | Sharpe | Ann Vol | Max Drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy & Hold | — | 10–50% (uncontrolled) | −135% (in crisis) |
| Vol-Target (HAR forecast) | improved | held near 15% target | −72% (in same crisis) |

The vol-targeted strategies held realized vol near the 15% target throughout, while buy-and-hold's vol swung between 10% and 50%. Peak drawdown roughly halved.

**Interpretation.** The value of a volatility forecast is *not* in picking direction — it is in stabilizing portfolio risk and reducing drawdowns. A decent vol forecast (even plain HAR, no decomposition) materially improves risk-adjusted outcomes through sizing alone.

**Constraint motivated.** This is the positive result the SRS risk-control modules are built around.

---

## III.6 Experiment 5 — Univariate Regime Detection

**Setup.** Rolling-window Hurst exponent (on returns, not price) plus coarse-band wavelet energy share, used to classify trend / mean-reverting / random-walk regimes on synthetic data with known alternating regimes. Thresholds calibrated causally from an initial random-walk window.

**Result.**

- Detectors recovered the true regime structure: Hurst rose into the trend regime, collapsed in the mean-reverting regime; energy share moved in agreement.
- Mean estimated Hurst by true regime: RW 0.47, Trend 0.78, MR 0.57 (trend detected cleanly; MR detection noisier).
- Combined-detector classification accuracy ≈ 64%; trend detected near-perfectly, MR moderately, RW hardest (often over-called as a regime).
- A regime-aware strategy beat buy-and-hold mainly by **standing aside during random-walk periods**, not by clever directional bets. The edge came from *not trading*, not from prediction.

**A critical bug surfaced.** The Hurst estimator must be applied to *stationary* input (returns), not to *non-stationary* input (price/log-price). Applied to price, the variance-decay slope blows up and the estimator returns garbage. This is the single most common error in applying wavelet-variance Hurst estimation.

**Interpretation.** Regime detection from decomposition works for *diagnosis*. As a trading tool it helps through selectivity (knowing when not to trade), not through forecasting.

**Constraints motivated.** Part I FR-HURST (input must be stationary), B-series notes on calibrating thresholds from data rather than theory (H=0.5 textbook boundary is unreliable in the presence of estimator noise).

---

## III.7 Experiment 6 — Continuous Regime Score for Sizing

**Setup.** Realistic stochastic-vol data with both drift regimes (trend up/down/sideways) and vol regimes (calm/turbulent). Five detectors (rolling Hurst, coarse-band energy, variance ratio, vol z-score, drift z-score) blended into a continuous −1..+1 exposure score driving position size. Compared against buy-and-hold and plain vol-targeting, 2bps cost.

**Result (OOS).**

| Strategy | Sharpe | Ann Ret | Max Drawdown | Avg Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buy & Hold | +0.57 | +14.5% | −59.9% | 1.00 |
| Vol-Target (no regime) | +0.51 | +8.3% | −42.3% | 0.87 |
| Regime-aware (aggressive, directional) | +0.24 | +2.0% | −28.0% | 0.23 |
| Vol-Target × Regime (conservative modulation) | +0.56 | +8.8% | −30.6% | 0.81 |

**Interpretation.** The aggressive directional use of the regime signal performed *worst* — on realistic data where drift is small relative to vol, the detectors are too noisy and lagging to time direction. The conservative use (modulate an already-sensible vol-target baseline) preserved Sharpe and cut max drawdown by ~25%. The difference vs plain vol-targeting was **not statistically significant** in return terms; the gain was confined to drawdown.

**Constraint motivated.** Use noisy regime signals defensively (to modulate a sound base strategy), never as standalone directional bets. Report drawdown, not just Sharpe.

## III.8 Experiment 7 — Vol-Regime Classification

**Setup.** Binary classification of "turbulent tomorrow" (next-day log-RV above a causal rolling 70th percentile) using logistic regression. Features: HAR-only vs HAR + MODWT bands + rolling Hurst. Walk-forward, refit every 60 days.

**Result (OOS, base rate 29% turbulent).**

| Model | Accuracy | Brier | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predict base-rate naively | 71.0% | 0.206 | — |
| HAR-only | 91.7% | 0.065 | 0.974 |
| HAR + MODWT + Hurst | 91.9% | 0.062 | 0.973 |

**Trading use (vol-regime-modulated sizing, 2bps cost):**

| Strategy | Sharpe | Max Drawdown |
|---|---|---|
| Vol-Target plain | +0.58 | −40.0% |
| VT × symmetric regime multiplier | +0.45 | −47.1% |
| VT × defensive (cut-only) multiplier | +0.51–0.54 | −32.6% to −34.8% |

**Interpretation.** Vol-regime classification is *genuinely good* (AUC 0.97) — this is the clearest positive result in the session, and a direct consequence of volatility's long memory. But the MODWT/Hurst features added almost nothing over HAR (Brier improved 4%, accuracy 0.2%). Converting the excellent classifier into trading edge was hard: symmetric amplification hurt (errors when levering up are costly), while asymmetric defensive use improved drawdown (~18% reduction) at a small cost in return. None of the trading differences vs plain vol-targeting were statistically significant. Plain vol-targeting already cuts exposure in turbulent periods because its trailing vol estimate rises naturally — by the time the classifier confirms turbulence, the baseline has already adapted.

**Constraint motivated.** Even an excellent forecast (AUC 0.97) need not produce trading edge over a simple adaptive baseline. Always compare to the baseline you'd actually run, not to buy-and-hold.

## III.9 Experiment 8 — Cross-Asset / Multivariate

**Setup.** Basket of 4 assets sharing a common volatility factor, with idiosyncratic shocks in a "leader" asset propagating to "followers" with lags of 3, 5, 8 days. Tested whether cross-asset features (the leader's recent vol) improve next-day vol-regime prediction over single-asset HAR.

**Result.**

| Asset | Role | AUC single-asset | AUC + leader features | Δ AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | leader | 0.979 | 0.979 | −0.000 |
| 1 | follower (lag 3) | 0.990 | 0.990 | −0.000 |
| 2 | follower (lag 5) | 0.986 | 0.986 | −0.000 |
| 3 | follower (lag 8) | 0.982 | 0.981 | −0.001 |

An isolated "early-warning" test — handicapping the follower's own features by freezing them stale by `lag` days, so they cannot have reacted yet — still showed negligible benefit from the leader's current vol (Δ AUC between −0.010 and +0.002).

**Interpretation.** Even with a structurally guaranteed lead-lag spillover, cross-asset features barely helped, because the spillover signal is small relative to each asset's own vol dynamics, and by the time a neighbor's stress is detectable, the asset's own vol is usually already reacting (markets are fast). Cross-asset edge in the real literature comes from *mechanical* leads (asynchronous trading hours, e.g. US close → Asia open), *causal* cascades (forced-seller liquidity chains), or *liquidity-tiered* factor stress (liquid CDS → illiquid cash bonds) — not from soft statistical co-movement.

**Constraint motivated.** Cross-asset/coherence features are promising in principle but rarely beat single-asset HAR except in mechanically- or causally-linked markets. Do not assume a multivariate model beats a univariate one; test it.

## III.10 Consolidated Verdict by Use Case

| Use case | Does decomposition help? | Honest verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Return-direction forecasting | No | Nothing survives causality; leakage produces fake edge |
| Volatility forecasting | Marginally | HAR captures ~all of it; decomposition adds <5% |
| Vol-targeting (risk sizing) | Indirectly | The big win; works with plain HAR, no decomposition needed |
| Univariate regime detection | For diagnosis | Helps drawdown via selectivity, not Sharpe |
| Vol-regime classification | Yes (AUC 0.97) | Excellent classifier; hard to monetize over adaptive baseline |
| Cross-asset prediction | Rarely | Single-asset HAR hard to beat outside mechanical leads |
| **Denoising for pattern detection** | **Yes** | **Genuine value — the Part II use case** |
| Multi-scale visualization / diagnostics | Yes | Genuine value — scalograms, coherence, energy ratios |
| Long-memory estimation (Hurst) | Yes | Genuine value — robust on stationary input |

## III.11 The Three Principles

**1. Causality is non-negotiable.** Any result that looks too good probably is. Build the leakage-detection test into the suite from day one: replace past target values with noise and confirm past predictions do not change.

**2. Always include a brutal benchmark.** Lag-returns for direction, HAR for volatility, random-walk null for pattern detection, and the strategy-you'd-actually-run (not buy-and-hold) for trading comparisons. If the elaborate method doesn't beat the simple one out-of-sample with significance, there is no result.

**3. Decomposition describes structure; it does not predict the next number.** Its honest, productive roles are denoising (before pattern detection), visualizing multi-scale structure, estimating long memory, detecting regimes for risk control, and modestly improving defensive position sizing. Asking it to forecast price direction is asking it to do the one thing it cannot.
