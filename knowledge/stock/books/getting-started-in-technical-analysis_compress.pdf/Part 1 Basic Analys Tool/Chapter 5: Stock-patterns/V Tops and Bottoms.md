# V Tops and Bottoms

## Definition
A sharp, instantaneous reversal with no consolidation or warning. Price turns on a dime.

## Analogy
A tennis ball thrown hard against a wall. Maximum velocity at impact, instant reversal. The problem: you can't tell a wall (permanent reversal) from a trampoline (bounce that continues) until afterward.

## The Core Problem
Indistinguishable from a sharp correction without supporting clues: spike, reversal day, wide-ranging day, or wide gap. The V bottom in Fig 5.31 had an extreme spike. The V top in Fig 5.30 had nothing — invisible in real time.

## Rules
- **#59**: V + nearby congestion → possible reversal. But if congestion breaks toward the V extreme → new lows/highs likely
- **#60**: V + multimonth consolidation near the reversal point → major top/bottom
- **#6**: Don't try to catch the V itself. Wait for subsequent flags for favorable entry

## Trading approach
Wait for the post-V consolidation (flag/pennant), then trade that with a defined stop. Catching the V requires luck; trading the confirmation requires discipline.

## Failed V → New Extreme
If the consolidation after a V bottom breaks downside and approaches the V extreme, treat it as a sign of impending new lows. Use the consolidation top as a stop for shorts.
