# Rounded Tops and Bottoms (Saucers)

## Definition
A smooth, gradual curvature of prices — no distinct peaks or troughs. The outer perimeter conforms to a rounding shape. Minor jags are acceptable.

## Analogy
A skateboard halfpipe viewed from the side. The skater rolls up one wall (prior trend), decelerates along the curved lip (the rounding), and rolls down the other side (new trend). Smooth, continuous transition — no sudden stops.

## Schwager's Assessment
> Occur somewhat infrequently, but are among the **most reliable** top and bottom formations.

This is one of the strongest endorsements of any pattern in the book.

## Key Criterion
"The main criterion is whether the **outer perimeter** conforms to a rounding shape." Minor internal jags don't disqualify it.

## Failed Rounding (Ch.11 — Breaking of Curvature)
A rounding top that is suddenly broken by a sharp upward move → the curvature is "broken" → bullish failed signal.

## Rule #74 (Cup and Handle)
A curved bottom + a shallower, same-direction curved consolidation near the top = bullish "cup-and-handle" formation.

## Detection Approach
Fit a quadratic (parabola) to swing-point extremes. Rounded bottom: positive quadratic coefficient (U-shape). Measure R-squared for fit quality. This is the most subjective formation — human eye still outperforms algorithms here.
