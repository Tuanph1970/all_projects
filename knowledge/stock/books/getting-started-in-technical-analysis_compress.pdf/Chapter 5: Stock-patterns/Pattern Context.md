# Pattern Context (Continuation vs. Reversal Interpretation)

## The Core Insight

The same geometric pattern means different things depending on where it appears:

> Chart analysis is more than just the recognition and interpretation of individual patterns. One of the earmarks of the successful chart trader is an ability to synthesize the various components of the overall picture. — Ch. 7

## Key Examples

**Triangles**: Mid-trend → continuation. After extended move → potential top/bottom. Same geometry, opposite interpretation.

**Flags near S/R**: Mid-trend → standard continuation. Near range top → enhanced bullish. Pulled back into range after failed breakout → reversal (Rule #57). Same shape, three different implications.

**Breakout direction vs. pattern type**: Schwager consistently prioritizes what the market *does* over what the shape *predicts*. A descending triangle that breaks up is bullish, regardless of what the textbook says.

## Multi-Timeframe Context (Ch. 7)

The Eurodollar example: the daily chart showed ambiguous trading-range breakouts. The weekly chart revealed a flag near the top of a five-year range — "extremely bullish," counseling against any daily sell signals.

## The Context Hierarchy

Most beginners evaluate geometry first and stop. Schwager evaluates context first:

1. **Macro context** (weekly/monthly) — long-term trend or range?
2. **Pattern location** relative to S/R — enhanced, standard, or inverted?
3. **Preceding move** magnitude/duration — continuation or reversal context?
4. **Pattern geometry** — what does the shape say? (evaluated LAST, not first)
