# Island Reversals (Island Tops, Island Bottoms)

## Definition
Price gaps in the trend direction, trades for 1+ days without filling the gap, then gaps in the **opposite direction**. The stranded price action between the two gaps is the "island."

## Analogy
A reconnaissance team crosses a bridge (gap in trend direction), scouts enemy territory for a few days (the island), retreats across a second bridge (gap opposite direction), and blows up both bridges. The territory was surveyed and rejected. Two destroyed bridges (unfilled gaps) = decisive abandonment.

## Potency
> Island reversals can often signal major trend transitions and should be given significant weight unless the gap is eventually filled.

## Critical Caveats
- False island reversals are **common** — second gap often fills within days
- Wait **3-5 days** after formation before concluding validity
- Signal remains in force as long as the **more recent gap** is NOT filled
- Trade-off: waiting = worse entry when valid

## Rule #70
An island reversal + pullback into the most recent trading range = possible major top/bottom signal.

## Island Duration Varies
- Single-day island (Fig 5.48) — most dramatic, clearest signal
- Multi-day island (Fig 5.49) — traded above initial gap for several days
- Multi-week island (Fig 5.51) — weeks between the two gaps

## Connection to One-Day Patterns
Island reversals are the multi-day version of [[Spike-Reversal Combination]]: climactic gap (like spike protrusion) + gap reversal (like reversal day close against trend). The double-gap structure compounds the significance.

## Connections
- [[Gaps]] — island reversals are fundamentally a gap-pair pattern
- [[Spike-Reversal Combination]] — the single-bar analog
- Can help distinguish exhaustion gaps from runaway gaps retroactively
