# Top and Bottom Formations

> These are the major reversal patterns — the formations that signal the end of significant trends. Unlike continuation patterns (which are pauses), these represent **transitions** from one regime to another.

## Prerequisites for All Formations

Every top/bottom formation shares one critical prerequisite:

> A valid [pattern] is formed only **after a major price move has occurred**. Patterns that bear the shape of [the formation] but lack this requirement can be misleading. — Schwager (on H&S, but applies universally)

Without a preceding trend, there's nothing to reverse. A "double top" in the middle of a sideways market is just a trading range with two bumps.

## The Confirmation vs. Anticipation Problem

This tension runs through every formation. Schwager's general observation:

- **Waiting for official confirmation** (neckline break, reaction low breach) is safer but often means giving back a large portion of the prior move
- **Anticipating the pattern** based on supporting evidence (spikes, flags, wide-ranging days) gives better entry prices but higher false-positive risk
- The skilled trader combines the formation with one-day patterns as "clues" to act before official confirmation

## Catalog of Eight Formations

---

### 1. V Tops and Bottoms

**Structure**: A sharp turn-on-a-dime reversal with no consolidation, no double test, no warning. Price simply reverses direction violently.

**Analogy**: A car hitting a wall at speed. No gradual deceleration, no gentle curve — just impact and bounce. The problem is distinguishing a wall hit from a pothole (a sharp correction within a continuing trend).

**Key problem**: V formations are nearly impossible to trade proactively because they give no advance warning. They're only identifiable in hindsight *unless* accompanied by other evidence.

**Supporting clues to confirm a V reversal**:
- Prominent [[Spikes|spike]] (the V bottom in Fig 5.31 had an extreme spike)
- Significant [[Reversal Days|reversal day]]
- [[Wide-Ranging Days|Wide-ranging day]] with close against the trend
- Wide [[Gaps|gap]]

Without these clues, a V formation is indistinguishable from a sharp correction.

**Rules #59, #60**:
- V top/bottom followed by a nearby congestion pattern → possible reversal formation. But if the congestion breaks in the original trend's direction and approaches the V extreme → new trend extreme likely
- V top/bottom followed by **multimonth consolidation** near the reversal point → major top/bottom formation

**Detection difficulty**: Very hard algorithmically. You're essentially detecting a spike or sharp reversal that sticks, which requires significant hindsight.

---

### 2. Double Tops and Bottoms

**Structure**: Two peaks (or troughs) at approximately the same price level, separated by a reaction. The peaks don't need to be exactly equal — "same general price vicinity" is sufficient.

**Analogy**: A swimmer trying to touch the ceiling of a pool. They push up, touch it, sink back, push up again, touch the same spot. Two attempts at the same level — the ceiling wins.

**Confirmation**: Officially completed when price closes below (above) the **reaction low (high)** between the two peaks (troughs). But Schwager notes this can be impractical when the reaction is deep — by the time confirmation occurs, half the move has been surrendered.

**Anticipation using other patterns**: In the deutsche mark example (Fig 5.32), the double top wasn't officially confirmed until price had surrendered nearly half the April-August advance. But a spike high at the second top + a flag pattern after the initial decline gave enough clues to conclude a double top was forming before official confirmation.

**After large price moves**: Double tops/bottoms that form after large price moves are "strong indicators of a major trend reversal."

**Failed double top/bottom (Ch.11)**: When price penetrates through a double top (breaking above both peaks), it's a powerful bullish signal. Schwager notes these provide "good signals but are relatively rare."

---

### 3. Triple Tops and Bottoms

**Structure**: Three peaks (or troughs) at approximately the same level. An extension of the double top/bottom concept.

**Schwager's treatment**: Brief — "Top and bottom formations with more repetitions occur rather infrequently, but would be interpreted in the same fashion."

The same confirmation logic applies (close beyond the lowest reaction point), and the same anticipation strategy works. The three-test pattern is rarer but, when it appears, the repeated failure at the same level is strong evidence of that level's significance.

---

### 4. Head and Shoulders

**Structure**: Three peaks where the middle one (head) is higher than the two flanking ones (shoulders). A neckline connects the reaction lows between the peaks.

**Analogy**: Three mountain peaks — the middle one tallest. The line connecting the valley floors between them is the neckline. The formation is complete when the path descends below the neckline on the far side.

**Critical rules**:
- NOT complete until the **neckline is penetrated**
- Must follow a **major price move** — shapes that look like H&S without a preceding trend are misleading
- Most common mistake: **premature anticipation** of the H&S formation

**Failed H&S (Ch.11)**: "Failed signals involving head-and-shoulders patterns are more common [than failed double tops] and often provide excellent trading indicators." Schwager's confirmation criterion for a failed H&S: price retraces beyond the **most recent shoulder**. For a failed H&S top, that means a close above the right shoulder's high.

**Tactical note**: Schwager suggests waiting for a retracement after a failed H&S confirmation before entering, accepting the trade-off that some profitable moves will be missed entirely.

---

### 5. Rounded Tops and Bottoms (Saucers)

**Structure**: A smooth, gradual curvature of prices — no distinct peaks or troughs, just a gentle arc transitioning from one trend direction to another.

**Analogy**: A skateboard ramp. The skater (price) rolls up one side (the prior trend), gradually decelerates along the curved surface (the rounding), and starts rolling down the other side (the new trend). The transition is smooth, not sudden.

**Schwager's assessment**: "Occur somewhat infrequently, but are **among the most reliable** top and bottom formations."

**Key criterion**: The outer perimeter conforms to a rounding shape. Minor jags within the arc are acceptable — it's the overall envelope that matters.

**Failed rounding (Ch.11 — Breaking of curvature)**: If what appeared to be a rounding top is suddenly broken by a sharp upward move, the curvature is "broken" and the rounding pattern becomes a failed signal — bullish.

---

### 6. Wedges

**Structure**: Prices edge steadily higher (rising wedge) or lower (declining wedge) in a **converging pattern where both lines slope in the same direction**. This is what distinguishes wedges from triangles — in a triangle, the lines slope toward each other from opposite directions.

**Analogy**: A rising wedge is like running uphill on a path that gets narrower. You're still moving forward (up), but each step covers less new ground. Eventually you can't squeeze through and you have to reverse.

**Behavioral logic (rising wedge)**: The inability to accelerate on the upside despite continued probes into new high ground suggests strong scale-up selling pressure. The sellers are leaning in harder at each new high.

**Signal**: Sell when price breaks below the lower wedge line (rising wedge). Buy when price breaks above the upper wedge line (declining wedge).

**Time horizon**: Wedges can take **years** to complete (Fig 5.47 — multiyear declining wedge in gold).

---

### 7. Triangles as Top/Bottom Formations

Already covered in the [[Triangles]] note, but the key point for this context: the **same triangle pattern** can be a continuation or a reversal depending on where it appears. After an extended trend, a triangle should be treated as a potential top/bottom formation, with the breakout direction determining interpretation.

---

### 8. Island Reversals

**Structure**: Price gaps in the direction of the trend, trades for one or more days without filling the gap, then gaps in the **opposite direction**. The "island" is the price action between the two gaps.

**Analogy**: A reconnaissance team that crosses a bridge (gap up), scouts the territory for a few days (the island), then retreats back across a second bridge (gap down), burning both bridges behind them. The territory was surveyed and rejected — the market tested the extension and said no.

**Potency**: "Island reversals can often signal major trend transitions and should be given significant weight unless the gap is eventually filled."

**Important caveats**:
- False island reversals are **common** — the second gap is often filled within the first few days
- Wait **3-5 days** after formation before concluding it's valid
- The signal remains in force as long as the **more recent gap** is not filled
- The trade-off: waiting for confirmation often means a worse entry

**Connection to one-day patterns**: Island reversals are relatives of [[Spikes]] and [[Reversal Days]] — they represent the climactic gap + gap-reversal combo, which is the multi-day version of the spike-reversal combination.

---

## How One-Day Patterns Act as "Clues" for Top/Bottom Formations

This is one of Schwager's most practical insights. Top/bottom formations often take weeks or months to complete, and official confirmation can come very late. One-day patterns serve as early evidence:

| One-Day Pattern | How it Supports a Top/Bottom Formation |
|----------------|---------------------------------------|
| [[Spikes]] | Spike at a peak/trough = climactic exhaustion → strengthens V top/bottom or double top/bottom thesis |
| [[Wide-Ranging Days]] | WRD with close against prior trend = momentum shift → early reversal evidence |
| [[Reversal Days]] | Strict reversal day at the formation's extreme → confirmation of buying/selling climax |
| [[Gaps]] | Exhaustion gap near the formation → trend exhaustion; breakaway gap in the new direction → confirms the reversal |
| [[Flags]] | Flag after initial reversal move → confirms the first leg of the new trend |

The deutsche mark double top example (Fig 5.32) is the best illustration: the double top itself wasn't confirmed for weeks, but a spike at the second top + a flag pointing down gave enough evidence to trade the reversal early.

## Failed Top/Bottom Formations (Chapter 11)

When price penetrates through what appeared to be a major top or bottom formation, the failure is a powerful signal in the **opposite direction**:

| Formation | Failure Confirmation | Significance |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------|
| Double top | Close above both peaks | Good signals, relatively rare |
| Double bottom | Close below both troughs | Good signals, relatively rare |
| H&S top | Close above the most recent shoulder | More common, excellent signals |
| H&S bottom | Close below the most recent shoulder | More common, excellent signals |
| Rounding top | Breaking of curvature (sharp move up) | Bullish |
| Rounding bottom | Breaking of curvature (sharp move down) | Bearish |

> A failed signal is more reliable than the original signal. — Rule #76

## Python: Top/Bottom Formation Detection Framework

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Optional


def find_swing_points(df: pd.DataFrame, order: int = 5) -> tuple:
    """Identify swing highs and lows (requires future data)."""
    swing_highs, swing_lows = [], []
    for i in range(order, len(df) - order):
        window_h = df['high'].iloc[i - order:i + order + 1]
        window_l = df['low'].iloc[i - order:i + order + 1]
        if df['high'].iloc[i] == window_h.max():
            swing_highs.append((i, df.index[i], df['high'].iloc[i]))
        if df['low'].iloc[i] == window_l.min():
            swing_lows.append((i, df.index[i], df['low'].iloc[i]))
    return swing_highs, swing_lows


def detect_double_tops(df: pd.DataFrame,
                       swing_order: int = 10,
                       tolerance_pct: float = 0.03,
                       min_reaction_pct: float = 0.03,
                       min_prior_move_bars: int = 30) -> list[dict]:
    """
    Detect double top patterns.

    Two swing highs at approximately the same level,
    separated by a reaction, preceded by an advance.
    """
    swing_highs, _ = find_swing_points(df, swing_order)

    patterns = []
    for i in range(len(swing_highs) - 1):
        idx1, date1, price1 = swing_highs[i]
        idx2, date2, price2 = swing_highs[i + 1]

        # Peaks at similar level
        if abs(price1 - price2) / price1 > tolerance_pct:
            continue

        # Reaction between peaks
        between = df.iloc[idx1:idx2 + 1]
        reaction_low = between['low'].min()
        reaction_depth = (min(price1, price2) - reaction_low) / min(price1, price2)
        if reaction_depth < min_reaction_pct:
            continue  # Too shallow — no real pullback

        # Preceding advance
        prior = df.iloc[max(0, idx1 - min_prior_move_bars):idx1]
        if len(prior) < 10:
            continue
        prior_low = prior['low'].min()
        prior_advance = (price1 - prior_low) / prior_low
        if prior_advance < 0.05:  # Need at least 5% advance
            continue

        # Confirmation: close below reaction low
        confirmed = False
        conf_date = None
        for j in range(idx2 + 1, min(idx2 + 30, len(df))):
            if df.iloc[j]['close'] < reaction_low:
                confirmed = True
                conf_date = df.index[j]
                break

        patterns.append({
            'type': 'double_top',
            'peak1_date': date1, 'peak1_price': price1,
            'peak2_date': date2, 'peak2_price': price2,
            'reaction_low': reaction_low,
            'reaction_depth_pct': reaction_depth,
            'confirmed': confirmed,
            'confirmation_date': conf_date,
        })

    return patterns


def detect_head_and_shoulders(df: pd.DataFrame,
                               swing_order: int = 10,
                               shoulder_tolerance_pct: float = 0.05
                               ) -> list[dict]:
    """
    Detect head-and-shoulders top patterns.

    Three swing highs where the middle (head) is highest,
    and the two shoulders are at approximately the same level.
    """
    swing_highs, _ = find_swing_points(df, swing_order)

    patterns = []
    for i in range(len(swing_highs) - 2):
        _, date_ls, price_ls = swing_highs[i]      # Left shoulder
        idx_h, date_h, price_h = swing_highs[i + 1]  # Head
        _, date_rs, price_rs = swing_highs[i + 2]    # Right shoulder

        # Head must be highest
        if price_h <= price_ls or price_h <= price_rs:
            continue

        # Shoulders at similar level
        if abs(price_ls - price_rs) / price_ls > shoulder_tolerance_pct:
            continue

        # Find neckline (lows between shoulders)
        left_idx = swing_highs[i][0]
        right_idx = swing_highs[i + 2][0]
        between1 = df.iloc[left_idx:idx_h + 1]
        between2 = df.iloc[idx_h:right_idx + 1]
        neck_left = between1['low'].min()
        neck_right = between2['low'].min()
        neckline = max(neck_left, neck_right)  # Conservative

        # Confirmation: close below neckline
        confirmed = False
        conf_date = None
        for j in range(right_idx + 1, min(right_idx + 30, len(df))):
            if df.iloc[j]['close'] < neckline:
                confirmed = True
                conf_date = df.index[j]
                break

        patterns.append({
            'type': 'head_and_shoulders_top',
            'left_shoulder': {'date': date_ls, 'price': price_ls},
            'head': {'date': date_h, 'price': price_h},
            'right_shoulder': {'date': date_rs, 'price': price_rs},
            'neckline': neckline,
            'confirmed': confirmed,
            'confirmation_date': conf_date,
        })

    return patterns


def detect_island_reversals(df: pd.DataFrame,
                             min_gap_atr: float = 0.5,
                             max_island_bars: int = 15,
                             atr_period: int = 14) -> list[dict]:
    """
    Detect island reversal patterns.

    Gap in trend direction + 1-N days + gap in opposite direction.
    """
    from breakout_confirmation import compute_true_range

    tr = compute_true_range(df)
    adtr = tr.rolling(atr_period).mean()

    # Find all gaps
    gaps = []
    for i in range(1, len(df)):
        curr, prev = df.iloc[i], df.iloc[i - 1]
        gap_size = 0
        direction = None
        if curr['low'] > prev['high']:
            gap_size = curr['low'] - prev['high']
            direction = 'up'
        elif curr['high'] < prev['low']:
            gap_size = prev['low'] - curr['high']
            direction = 'down'
        if direction and not pd.isna(adtr.iloc[i]):
            if gap_size / adtr.iloc[i] >= min_gap_atr:
                gaps.append({'idx': i, 'date': df.index[i],
                             'direction': direction, 'size': gap_size})

    # Find gap pairs (up then down, or down then up)
    islands = []
    for i in range(len(gaps) - 1):
        g1 = gaps[i]
        for j in range(i + 1, len(gaps)):
            g2 = gaps[j]
            bar_gap = g2['idx'] - g1['idx']
            if bar_gap > max_island_bars:
                break
            if g1['direction'] != g2['direction']:  # Opposite gaps
                island_type = ('island_top' if g1['direction'] == 'up'
                               else 'island_bottom')
                islands.append({
                    'type': island_type,
                    'gap1_date': g1['date'], 'gap1_dir': g1['direction'],
                    'gap2_date': g2['date'], 'gap2_dir': g2['direction'],
                    'island_bars': bar_gap,
                    'note': 'Wait 3-5 days; check if gap2 is filled',
                })
                break  # Take first matching pair

    return islands


def check_formation_failure(df: pd.DataFrame,
                             formation: dict,
                             lookforward: int = 30) -> dict | None:
    """
    Check if a top/bottom formation is penetrated (Ch. 11).

    For double tops: close above both peaks
    For H&S tops: close above the most recent shoulder
    """
    if formation['type'] == 'double_top':
        peak = max(formation['peak1_price'], formation['peak2_price'])
        start_idx = df.index.get_loc(formation['peak2_date'])
        for i in range(start_idx + 1,
                       min(start_idx + lookforward, len(df))):
            if df.iloc[i]['close'] > peak:
                return {
                    'failure_date': df.index[i],
                    'failure_type': 'penetration_of_double_top',
                    'implied_direction': 'bullish',
                    'note': 'Powerful but rare signal (Ch.11)',
                }

    elif formation['type'] == 'head_and_shoulders_top':
        rs_price = formation['right_shoulder']['price']
        rs_date = formation['right_shoulder']['date']
        start_idx = df.index.get_loc(rs_date)
        for i in range(start_idx + 1,
                       min(start_idx + lookforward, len(df))):
            if df.iloc[i]['close'] > rs_price:
                return {
                    'failure_date': df.index[i],
                    'failure_type': 'failed_head_and_shoulders',
                    'implied_direction': 'bullish',
                    'note': ('More common than failed double tops, '
                             'excellent signals. Consider waiting '
                             'for retracement before entering.'),
                }

    return None
```
