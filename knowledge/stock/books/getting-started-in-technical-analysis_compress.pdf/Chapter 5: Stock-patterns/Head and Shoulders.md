# Head and Shoulders (Top and Bottom)

## Definition
Three peaks where the middle (head) is higher than the two flanking ones (shoulders). A neckline connects the reaction lows between the peaks. H&S bottom is the mirror.

## Analogy
Three mountain peaks seen from a distance — the middle one tallest. The hiker descends from the right shoulder, and when they drop below the valley floor (neckline), they've left the mountain range.

## Critical Rules
1. NOT complete until **neckline penetrated** — see [[Neckline Penetration]]
2. Must follow a **major price move** — shapes without preceding trends are misleading
3. Most common novice mistake: **premature anticipation**

## Why Premature Anticipation Fails
After left shoulder + head, it's tempting to label the next rally as the right shoulder. But many uptrends produce three peaks where the middle is highest — that's just a healthy trend. H&S is only valid when the *complete structure* forms and the neckline breaks.

## Failed H&S (Ch.11)
"Failed signals involving head-and-shoulders patterns are **more common** than failed double tops and often provide **excellent trading indicators**."

Confirmation for failed H&S top: close above the **right shoulder**.

Schwager suggests waiting for a retracement after the failure signal before entering — accepting that some sharp moves will be missed.
