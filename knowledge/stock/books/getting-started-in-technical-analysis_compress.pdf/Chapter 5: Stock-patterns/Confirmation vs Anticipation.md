# Confirmation vs. Anticipation (Entry Timing)

## The Spectrum

Every pattern creates a timing decision:

```
ANTICIPATE ◄────────────────────► CONFIRM
Enter during       Enter on        Wait for confirmed
formation          breakout        breakout

Better price       Standard        Worst price
Higher false       Standard        Lowest false
positive rate      risk            positive rate
```

## Schwager's Preferences

**Flags/pennants**: Anticipate — enter during formation. Probability already favors continuation, and waiting doesn't materially improve accuracy.

**H&S**: Do NOT anticipate. Premature anticipation is "one of the most common mistakes made by novice chartists."

**Double tops with deep reaction**: Anticipate using clue-stacking (spike at peak 2 + flag after initial decline). Waiting for official confirmation surrenders too much profit.

**Island reversals**: Wait 3-5 days. False islands are common.

## The Clue-Stacking Resolution

Rather than choosing one extreme, stack multiple independent evidence types to justify earlier action:

1. Pattern structure (the macro framework)
2. One-day pattern clues at critical points (micro evidence)
3. Context relative to S/R (environmental amplifier)
4. Follow-through behavior (confirmation evidence)

More aligned clues → earlier justified entry. See [[Pattern Combinations]].

## The Deep-Reaction Problem

For double tops with deep reactions (Fig 5.32), confirmation is *structurally impractical* — half the move is gone by the time it arrives. This forces anticipation. For shallow reactions (Fig 5.33), confirmation is cheap — safe to wait.

## Rule #6
> When looking for a major reversal, wait for some pattern that suggests timing is right rather than fading the trend at projected objectives. In most cases the market will not form V-type reversals — prices will pull back to test highs and lows, often a number of times.
