# Wedges (Rising and Declining)

## Definition
Prices edge steadily in one direction within converging lines that **both slope the same way**. The critical distinction from triangles: in a triangle, lines slope toward each other from opposite directions; in a wedge, both go the same way but at different rates.

## Analogy
A **rising wedge** is like running uphill on a path that narrows with each step. You're still climbing, but each stride covers less ground. Eventually the path closes and you turn back.

## Behavioral Logic (Rising Wedge)
> The inability of prices to accelerate on the upside despite continued probes into new high ground suggests the existence of strong scale-up selling pressure.

Each new high is met by harder selling. The range narrows because buyers win each battle by less.

## Signals
- Rising wedge → sell on break below lower wedge line
- Declining wedge → buy on break above upper wedge line

## Time Horizon
Can take **years** to complete (Fig 5.47 — multiyear declining wedge in gold). Among the longest-forming patterns.

## Structural Distinction from Triangles

| | Wedge | Triangle |
|---|---|---|
| Upper line | Slopes same direction as lower | Slopes opposite to lower |
| Lower line | Slopes same direction as upper | Slopes opposite to upper |
| Both lines | Both up (rising) or both down (declining) | One up, one down (converging) |
| Implication | Trend exhaustion → reversal | Consolidation → usually continuation |

## Connections
- [[Triangles]] — similar converging structure, different slope geometry
- Longest time horizon of any formation in the catalog
