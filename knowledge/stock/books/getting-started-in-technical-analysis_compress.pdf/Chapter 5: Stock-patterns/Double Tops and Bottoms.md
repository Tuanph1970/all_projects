# Double Tops and Bottoms

## Definition
Two peaks (or troughs) at approximately the same price level, separated by a reaction. Peaks need not be exactly equal — "same general price vicinity" is sufficient.

## Analogy
A swimmer trying to touch the ceiling of a pool. Push up, touch it, sink back, push up again, touch the same spot. The ceiling wins.

## Completion
Close below (above) the **reaction low (high)** between the two peaks. But this can be impractical when the reaction is deep — see [[Double Top-Bottom Completion]].

## Clue-stacking for early anticipation
In the deutsche mark example (Fig 5.32): spike high at second top + flag pattern after initial downswing = enough evidence to trade before official confirmation.

## After large prior moves
"Double tops and bottoms that materialize after large price moves should be viewed as strong indicators of a major trend reversal."

## Failed double top (Ch.11)
Penetration through both peaks → powerful bullish signal. "Good signals but relatively rare." The immense rally following the coffee double-top penetration (Fig 11.28) illustrates the power.
