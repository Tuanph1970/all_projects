# Chapter 4 — Trading Ranges and Support and Resistance

> *Source: Jack D. Schwager — Getting Started in Technical Analysis*

## Overview

This chapter covers the foundational concepts of **sideways markets** (trading ranges) and the **horizontal price levels** (support and resistance) that govern price behavior. The core insight is that markets spend most of their time moving sideways, and the key to profitability lies in recognizing when a range ends and a trend begins.

## Key Takeaways

1. Markets spend most of their time in trading ranges — avoid trading them unless using oscillators.
2. Breakouts from ranges are the high-probability trading signals, but they require confirmation.
3. Support and resistance are **zones**, not exact prices.
4. Once a level is broken, its role **reverses** (ceiling becomes floor, floor becomes ceiling).
5. Clusters of prior highs/lows create stronger barriers than single peaks.
6. Dynamic tools (envelope bands) adapt with price but fail during extended trends.
